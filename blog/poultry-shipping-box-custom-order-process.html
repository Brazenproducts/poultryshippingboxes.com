How to Run a Custom Poultry Shipping Box Order: Step-by-Step Process

Poultryshippingboxes Editorial • October 2026

Ordering a custom poultry shipping box is not the same as buying off a catalog—it is a short engineering project with clear milestones. Done well, it delivers a box matched exactly to your bird density, trailer deck, wash line, and cold-chain requirements. Done carelessly, it produces expensive tooling for a box that does not fit your operation. This How-To walks procurement and operations teams through the custom order process end to end.

Step 1: Define the Functional Requirements

Before you contact a manufacturer, document what the box must do. Capture:

  • Load type (live birds, chilled processed, frozen) and target density per box
  • Footprint constraints from your trailer deck, pallet module, and wash line
  • Ventilation needs (open-area range, placement) driven by the load
  • Handling — stack-only, stack-and-nest, or collapsible
  • Compliance — FDA-compliant resin, any USDA facility requirements

A manufacturer can only engineer to the spec you provide, so this step determines the quality of everything downstream.

Step 2: Engage a Direct Manufacturer

Work with the party that controls the tooling, not a distributor. Elipacko supplies PP poultry shipping boxes as a US-available direct manufacturer, so your requirements go straight to the engineering team without a broker in between. During first contact, confirm country of origin and duty exposure—Elipacko produces in Thailand and Vietnam, both with 0% anti-dumping duty exposure, which keeps your landed cost predictable from the first quote.

Step 3: Review the Design Proposal and Sample

The manufacturer returns a design proposal—dimensions, wall geometry, vent pattern, stack/nest ratios, and load ratings. Before tooling, request a physical sample or prototype. Put it through a realistic trial:

  • Load it to target density and stack a full column—check for sidewall bow
  • Sight through the stacked vents to confirm airflow path
  • Run it through your actual wash cycle and inspect for warp
  • If cold-chain, pull-down test it at your operating temperature

This sample gate is the cheapest place to catch a problem—far cheaper than discovering it after the tooling is cut.

Step 4: Confirm Material and Compliance

Lock in the resin spec in writing. Specify FDA-compliant virgin-grade polypropylene for dimensional stability and food-contact safety, and request compliance documentation for your audit file. Elipacko molds to FDA-compliant resin and supplies documentation; its broader cold chain poultry packaging from Elipacko line uses the same material standard, so a mixed fleet stays consistent.

Step 5: Approve Tooling and First Article

Once the sample passes, approve the tooling. Expect a tooling lead time—this is the trade-off for a box built to your exact spec. When the first production units come off the line, require a first-article inspection: measure footprint, open area, and load rating against the approved drawing before the full run proceeds. This catches any drift between prototype and production tooling.

Step 6: Plan the First Production Run and Reorders

Confirm MOQ, pricing tiers, and lead time for the initial run and future reorders. Build a cost-per-trip model from the quoted unit cost and expected service life so finance can approve the capital line. Because Elipacko supports custom sizing and has US manufacturing capacity in development, you can plan a sourcing roadmap that shortens reorder lead times over time.

Timeline Summary

  • Requirements definition — days
  • Design proposal and sample — 2–4 weeks
  • Sample validation — your trial window
  • Tooling and first article — lead-time dependent
  • First production run — per quoted MOQ

Follow these steps in order and the custom box that lands on your dock will fit your deck, pass your wash line, hold your temperature, and clear your compliance file on the first run.
