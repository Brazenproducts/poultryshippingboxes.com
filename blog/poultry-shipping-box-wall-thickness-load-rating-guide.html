Poultry Shipping Box Spec Guide: Wall Thickness and Load Ratings

Poultryshippingboxes Editorial • November 2026

Specifying Poultry Shipping Boxes by the Numbers

Buying poultry shipping boxes on price alone is how operations end up with crushed loads, rejected shipments, and crates that fail audit. The specifications that actually govern performance, wall thickness, gauge, and verified load ratings, rarely appear on a basic quote. This guide gives procurement and logistics teams the vocabulary and benchmarks to write a spec that guarantees boxes survive the stack height, humidity, and cold-chain cycling of real poultry distribution.

Wall Thickness and Why It Governs Stacking Strength

For twin-wall PP shipping boxes, wall thickness is expressed in millimeters of panel, commonly 3mm, 4mm, or 5mm, and in grammage, the weight of material per square meter. A 3mm, 450 gsm board suits light single-trip applications; heavy reusable poultry service typically calls for 4-5mm board at 700-1,000 gsm. Thicker, heavier board resists the panel bowing that leads to column collapse when boxes are stacked six or eight high on a pallet. When you evaluate PP poultry shipping boxes, ask for both the millimeter thickness and the grammage, because two boxes with identical outer dimensions can differ 40 percent in crush strength based on board weight alone.

Reading Load Ratings Correctly

Load ratings come in two flavors that are easy to confuse. Dynamic load is what one box can carry while being moved or handled. Static stacking load is what the bottom box in a column bears over time in a warehouse. The static figure is almost always the binding constraint for poultry distribution, where boxes sit stacked in coolers for hours. Demand a static load rating measured after humidity conditioning, since PP holds its strength in moisture far better than fiberboard but connector and corner design still matter.

  • Light duty: up to 15 kg static per box, single or limited reuse.
  • Medium duty: 15-30 kg static, standard reusable poultry service.
  • Heavy duty: 30 kg and above, deep-stack and automated handling.

Ventilation Versus Structural Integrity

Poultry boxes need ventilation for air exchange and heat dissipation, but every vent slot removes material and reduces panel strength. Good box design concentrates venting in low-stress zones and reinforces the corners and top rails that carry stacking load. A spec that demands maximum ventilation without addressing where the vents sit will produce boxes that breathe well and crush early. The engineering trade-off between airflow and strength is exactly where a capable manufacturer earns its place.

Dimensional Tolerance and Pallet Fit

Boxes that vary even a few millimeters in footprint stack unevenly and waste pallet cube. Specify a dimensional tolerance, typically plus or minus 2mm, and confirm the footprint modularizes cleanly to your pallet, whether 48x40 inch or 1200x1000mm. Boxes engineered to full-pallet modularity eliminate the overhang that invites edge crushing in transit. This is where custom sizing beats off-the-shelf; a box built to your exact pallet dimension uses every square inch.

Material Grade and Compliance

For direct food contact and USDA facility use, the resin grade is non-negotiable. Specify FDA-compliant virgin or food-grade PP rather than mixed regrind, which can introduce contaminants and inconsistent wall strength. The difference shows up in both compliance audits and service life. Buyers who need documentation should request material certs up front rather than after a rejection.
